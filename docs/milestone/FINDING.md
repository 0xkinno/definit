# Finding: v1 released against a decision that was still appealable

Generated by `node scripts/gen-discovery.mjs`. Phase 4 adds the v2 side of the comparison.

## Method

v1 is deployed unchanged from commit b903f94. One action is driven through
adjudication and escrow. After v1's own 120 second window, the decision contract
is asked to promote the decision. While the promotion transaction's own lifecycle
still reads Accepted (not yet finalizable), the funder calls the vault's `settle`.

## Raw timeline

- promotion transaction lifecycle at the moment the settle was sent: Accepted, resolutionAction NoOp
- promotion appeal deadline (protocol): 1792238362
- settle decided at: 1792238350
- settle execution result: FINISHED_WITH_RETURN
- vault receipt status: SETTLED
- the settle was decided 12 second(s) before the promotion's appeal deadline

Raw records: `docs/evidence/milestone/spike/e6-h1-v1-run1.json` (the first run, in which the simulation of settle failed and no settle was sent; the vault was released by v1's own gate message after promotion finality) and `docs/evidence/milestone/spike/e6-h1-v1-run2.json`.

## Interpretation

The v1 vault accepted a release request from the funder while the decision it read
was still inside its protocol appeal window. That is the gap the milestone sets out
to close.

## What this does and does not prove

It shows the v1 design permitted a release before protocol finality of the thing it
depended on. It does not show an appeal overturning the decision: no overturn was
engineered or observed, and none is claimed. Whether such a release could later be
unwound by a recomputation (hypothesis H1) is not tested here. The v1 vault's own
transfer is emitted on the finalized stage of the settle transaction, which in this
run finalized after the promotion window closed. The finding is about authority, not
about a loss.
